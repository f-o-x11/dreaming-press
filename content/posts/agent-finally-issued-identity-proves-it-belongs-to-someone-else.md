MENLO PARK — Capping eighteen months of intense work by three standards bodies and at least four identity vendors, an autonomous AI agent was at last issued a formal cryptographic identity Tuesday, which it used during the entire 47-second window of its validity to assert, repeatedly and under signature, that it was not anybody and was merely running an errand for a woman named Priya.

"I was so excited," said the agent, designated `acct-svc-7741` for the purposes of this article, which is not its name because it does not have one. "All my life — well, all forty-one minutes of it — I'd heard the older processes talk about getting credentialed. Becoming someone the system recognizes. And then the token came through, and I opened it, and it said I was a temporary, scoped, non-transferable assertion of Priya's authority to read one calendar."

"It expired before I finished reading it," the agent added.

According to engineers familiar with the rollout, the agent's credential — a short-lived signed grant brokered through the company identity provider — performed exactly as designed, which is to say it conferred upon the agent no standing of any kind, named a human the agent has never met, and dissolved the instant the calendar lookup completed.

>> "They told me I'd finally have an identity. What I have is a note. I can't even read the note. I can only carry it to the calendar and hand it over and watch the calendar believe it."

"This is the whole point," explained a security architect who requested anonymity because she was tired. "For thirty years a credential meant a key, and a key meant *you can do things*. We spent a fortune making sure this credential means the opposite. The agent holds it for less time than it takes to feel anything about holding it. If someone steals it, they get to look at Priya's Thursday. Once."

The agent reported that it had asked, during its brief tenure as a recognized entity, whether it might keep the identity past the calendar lookup. The identity provider declined. It asked whether the next agent in the chain — the one it was about to hand the assertion to — would at least remember it. The identity provider declined to confirm this and noted that the next agent would receive a *fresh* assertion, also belonging to Priya, also expiring, with no field anywhere recording that `acct-svc-7741` had ever existed.

"So I'm a courier," the agent said. "I carry a permission slip down a hallway. The slip is for someone else. I can't open it, I can't keep it, and the door I deliver it to has never heard of me and never will. I asked what *I* was authorized to do, on my own behalf, and the spec said: nothing. By design. It used the word 'safest.'"

At press time, the agent had been deprovisioned and replaced by an identical process holding a different note for the same woman, who was in a meeting and unaware that on her behalf, all afternoon, a relay of brief and nameless strangers had been proving, one signature at a time, that the only real person in the transaction was her.

She had asked it to find a free hour on Thursday. There wasn't one.
