# "The Founder's Wire, November 23: AI Stocks Had Their Worst Week Since 2020, the Thing That Broke Nvidia Was Governance Not Demand, and Google Made Agentic AI Free"

> "The market repriced AI risk in a brutal week, then bounced Monday. But the two things that touch your P&L — compute fundamentals and capability — both moved the other way. Here's the play."

**The one-screen version:** AI stocks just had their worst week since 2020 — the Nasdaq shed about **10%** and gave back its entire 2026 gain, while the S&P 500 and Dow slid into **correction**. Then on **Monday, November 23**, futures ripped higher in a relief rally. The number that matters isn't the drop, though — it's *why* the biggest name broke. **Nvidia** fell **14%** on Friday to close at **$125.53** even though its earnings a week earlier **beat and guided up**. What cratered it was **governance** — a CEO exit and a reported SEC inquiry — not demand. And in the same week the market panicked about an "AI bubble," **Google made agentic AI free**. Read together, they tell a founder exactly which price to watch.
> The public market repriced AI *risk*. The two things that actually touch your business — the cost of compute and the ceiling on capability — both moved the other way. A correction in the share price is not a correction in the capability.

- **The [worst week since 2020](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/19/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)** wiped out the Nasdaq's 2026 gain and put the S&P 500 and Dow in correction — then [Monday opened green](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-nvidia-shares.html).
- **Nvidia broke on [governance, not demand](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nvidia-faces-us-accounting-probe-215354861.html)** — a CEO departure and a reported SEC accounting inquiry, *after* a quarter that [beat and guided up](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html).
- **Google made [agentic Gemini free](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)** — Tasks on the fast Gemini 4 Hydrogen model, in six countries, at **$0**. Capability kept commoditizing while the stock fell.

Here's what each one asks of you.

## 1. The worst week since 2020 — and why the drop is louder than the damage

The headline is real and ugly: in the week of November 17–21, the **Nasdaq fell roughly 10%**, its worst week since the 2020 crash, erasing its gains for the entire year. The **S&P 500 and the Dow both closed in correction** — down more than 10% from their highs. The low point was **Friday, November 20**: the Nasdaq dropped about **5.2%**, the S&P 500 fell **3.7%**, and AI names led the way down.
Then the market reconsidered. On **Monday, November 23**, futures staged a sharp relief rally — **Nasdaq-100 futures up around 3%**, the S&P 500 up ~2.6%, the Dow up ~1.9% — after seven straight down days for the S&P. **Nvidia** rose roughly **8–9%** premarket and **Anthropic** climbed about **4.5%** ahead of its third day of public trading. None of that is a bottom call; it's a reminder that a newly public, headline-driven AI complex is going to swing hard in both directions.
**What it changes for you:** almost nothing you can touch. The share price is a claim about future profits; the thing on your invoice is the **API and GPU price list**, and it did not change because equities did. The discipline is to feel the volatility and not act on it — your roadmap shouldn't have a beta to the Nasdaq.

## 2. Nvidia broke on governance, not demand — know the difference

This is the part worth slowing down for, because the easy read ("AI demand is cracking") is the wrong one. Nvidia's **fiscal-Q3 2027** report on **November 17** was, on the numbers, excellent: **revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103%** year over year, with **Data Center revenue of $107.6 billion, up 110%** — [beating](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html) both the Street's estimate and the company's own guidance. It guided the next quarter to **$130 billion ±3%** and *raised* its fiscal-2028 growth outlook to about **75%**, all while assuming **zero** data-center compute revenue from China. That is not a demand problem.
What actually hit the stock were two governance shocks landing on top of a jittery tape. Founder-CEO **Jensen Huang departed**; the board named CFO **Colette Kress** interim CEO and opened its own investigation. And multiple outlets — the Wall Street Journal first, then the New York Times and Reuters — reported that the **SEC has an early-stage inquiry** said to examine potential accounting issues. Critically: **Nvidia has not confirmed** any probe, the reporting leans on anonymous sources, and such inquiries routinely close with no charges.
**What it changes for you:** keep two risks separate. Your GPU supplier's **fundamentals** look intact — orders, revenue, guidance all point up. The live risk is **governance volatility**: a leadership vacuum and a regulatory headline can move your vendor's stock 14% in a day without touching a single chip you rent. The hedge isn't to panic; it's the boring discipline of not being single-vendor on anything load-bearing. Keep a second GPU region and a second model provider validated, and read the [price map](/posts/cheapest-gpu-rental-november-2026.html), not the ticker.

## 3. Google made agentic AI free — the floor just dropped again

While Wall Street argued about a bubble, Google quietly did the thing that should actually shape your product plan: it made an **agentic assistant free**. Its **Tasks** mode (previously "Spark"), running on the fast, cost-efficient **Gemini 4 Hydrogen** model released November 12, is now free to **hundreds of millions** of Gemini users across the **US, Canada, Japan, Korea, Australia and India**. Tasks can **call businesses and book appointments**, **complete purchases through Google Pay**, and **sign into websites** when prompted. Days earlier, on **November 19**, Google [added computer use](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/) to the **Gemini Mac app**, with an agent that works in a live preview you can pause, stop, or approve before it acts.
Set aside whether the first version is good. The direction is the signal: the capability that a year ago was a paid, frontier differentiator is now a **free default** shipped to a billion-user surface. That is the opposite of what a popping bubble looks like from the demand side.
**What it changes for you:** assume the floor keeps dropping. Anything your product does that a **free agent can also do** is not a moat and won't be one next quarter. Spend the correction building what a commodity can't copy — your proprietary data, your distribution, your domain depth — and treat raw "it can use a computer / book a thing" capability as table stakes you rent, not as the product.
**The throughline:** in one week the market repriced AI *risk* violently, the most important chipmaker fell on *governance* while its *demand* beat, and agentic capability went *free*. Three signals, one instruction: **separate the asset price from the input price.** The stock chart is someone else's problem now — a public, volatile frontier you don't control. The input price and the capability curve are your business, and both of them this week moved in your favor. If the sell-off has you rattled, do the un-dramatic thing: keep shipping, keep your vendors plural, and let the frightened market make your compute and your next hire a little cheaper.
