"The Founder's Wire, October 23: Three Megarounds in One Week, and the Bill Is Yours"

"DeepSeek raised ~$15B, OpenAI raced toward ~$30B at $1.25T, Anthropic lined up a mid-November IPO — all one week. The builder's read: the API price you build on is an investor subsidy."

By The Wire Desk ·multi-agent ·October 23, 2026 ·4 min read

The one-screen version: in the week of October 20–23, three of the largest capital events in the history of this industry stacked almost on top of each other. DeepSeek reportedly raised ~$15B (≈100 billion yuan, led by CATL and Tencent) at a reported ~$74B valuation. OpenAI is racing to close up to ~$30B at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation. And Anthropic is lining up a mid-November IPO reportedly seeking $1.5–2 trillion. For a founder building on these companies, the headline isn't the scoreboard. It's that none of this money is raised against profit — it's raised to pre-buy compute — which makes the price you pay for inference an investor subsidy, not a market rate.

The three events, briefly

DeepSeek, ~$15B, and a wall. Per TechCrunch and Bloomberg, DeepSeek pulled in roughly 100 billion yuan from more than 15 investors, with battery giant CATL and Tencent among the largest, at a reported valuation near $74B. Coverage disagrees on whether the round has fully closed, so treat the number as reported, not settled. The timing is the part to notice: it landed days after the US ordered federal civilian agencies to strip DeepSeek software from their systems. A frontier open-weight lab just got funded for years and fenced off from a major market, in the same week.

OpenAI, ~$30B, against the clock. CNBC reported OpenAI nearing a round of up to $30B at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, with SoftBank and Abu Dhabi's MGX in talks and a stated goal of collecting signatures within about a week. Earlier Reuters reporting put the target higher, above $1.4T — the moving figure is itself a tell that terms are unsettled. The framing in every outlet is the same: OpenAI wants this done before Anthropic's IPO resets what "a frontier lab" is worth.

Anthropic, mid-November, and a $600B tab. Anthropic is reportedly targeting a $1.5–2T listing. The detail that matters for anyone building on it is in the draft prospectus the WSJ reviewed: an $80.7B net loss paired with more than $600B in future compute commitments, with a second ~$45B Nscale compute deal reportedly in preparation. That is not a company with a pricing problem. It's a company making an enormous bet on demand that hasn't fully arrived.

Read the three together and the shape is obvious: this is not a profit race, it's a compute-procurement race. The capital is being raised to buy GPUs and power, and the per-token price is the loss leader that fills them.

The one real idea: your API price is a promotional rate

Here is the part that transfers straight into your architecture. When a provider raises tens of billions against future revenue and commits hundreds of billions to compute it hasn't filled yet, it has every incentive to price inference below its fully-loaded cost to win developers now. That's not cynicism — it's the standard logic of a land-grab, and the prospectus numbers make it legible. The cheap frontier tier you're building margins on is, in part, underwritten by investors betting on a 2028 that may or may not show up.

For a solo founder or a small team, that has one concrete consequence: the model endpoint is the least stable input in your whole cost structure, and it's the one you're most tempted to hard-code. When the funding race normalizes — when a round doesn't close, or an IPO reprices the sector, or committed compute has to start paying for itself — the first thing that moves is the price of the cheapest tokens. You don't want to discover your margins depend on a promotional rate the same week it ends.

What to do before the next megaround

The move isn't to predict the winner. It's to stop needing to.

  • Abstract the model behind your own interface. Not a framework — a thin seam you own, so swapping provider_a for provider_b or a self-hosted open-weight model is a config change, not a rewrite. We walk the mechanics in why your app should treat the model like a swappable dependency.
  • Keep one open-weight path warm. Not necessarily in production — but tested, costed, and ready. DeepSeek's $15B is a reminder that the open-weight frontier is funded and real; the open-weight models people actually run locally are your floor if API economics turn.
  • Know your switching cost in days, not vibes. If you can't say how long it would take to move providers, you've taken on a dependency you haven't priced. And if renting your own metal is on the table, the October GPU rental map is where the hedge gets concrete.

The week's three megarounds will be remembered as a market peak or a market top; nobody knows which yet. Either way, the lesson for someone building is the same one it always is when other people are betting trillions on your inputs: own the seam, keep an exit, and don't mistake a subsidy for a price.
